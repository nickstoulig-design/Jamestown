In May 1607, one hundred and four English men and boys came ashore on a marshy peninsula in Virginia. They were told to find gold, a passage to the Pacific, and a profit for their investors. Nobody told them how to eat.

You are in charge. Every season you decide how the colony spends its labor, and the fort in front of you changes to match. Eleven seasons stand between you and the spring of 1610.

History's answer: of roughly 500 people who reached Jamestown in those three years, about 60 were alive in the spring of 1610. See if you can do better.

Before the ships sail, the Virginia Company lets you fill the berths. One hundred and four men and boys. Every one of them is either a pair of useful hands or another mouth at the table.
